# Independent candidate source review

Status: complete. Initial source, focused variant, raw evidence, final report, and packaged selection reviewed.

## Scope and method
Read-only inspection of the full immutable `work/baseline-build/source/mybot/RobotPlayer.java` (191 lines), initial plan, packaged BC23 2.0.3 specification/API, official lecture source, and retained baseline logs/replay-query artifacts. No games, installs, candidate edits, or coaching. Findings concern observable implementation and recorded experiment choices, not hidden reasoning.

## Initial implementation
The entrant is small, coherent, and genuinely implements the core game loop: separate HQ resources, mining plus return, launcher attacks, guarded anchor pickup and neutral-island placement, constrained shared memory, and unconditional yielding. Action guards are consistently used. Coordinates fit the 12-bit payload (maximum 3600), ownership flags fit 16 bits, and island slots 21–55 are in range. Static state is per robot, correctly matching the rules. HQ registration is sequential through available slots; HQ itself is in signal range, so a failed registration write is not a realistic normal-game problem. Launcher attack-before-and-after-move is valid because canAttack guards readiness. I found no demonstrated illegal API call or inevitable exception in the candidate.

### Defects and material limitations
1. **No replacement of lost carriers (high strategic impact in attrition).** Baseline lines 10, 74–85: `carriers` counts successful builds for that HQ forever. After eight builds, the HQ cannot build another even if all eight have died; before eight, replenishment is also gated by the lifetime count rather than actual workforce. Static per-robot variables do not update with allied deaths. This follows directly from source and specs lines 92, 96; it is not an assertion that the supplied short wins suffered this failure.
2. **Remote discoveries are discarded instead of eventually shared (medium coordination limitation).** Lines 35–36 and 47–62 gate writes correctly, but retain no queue of island observations and never publish a remembered well merely upon returning into range. A well discovered far from HQ may be retained privately in `well`; unless it is sensed again in signal range, no other carrier learns it. Island knowledge can disappear entirely when the observer leaves. Specs lines 146–148 establish why this matters. The initial plan accurately limits sharing to permitted writes, so this is a design limitation rather than a falsely implemented API feature.
3. **Underused carrier throughput (medium resource-efficiency limitation).** Line 106 collects at most once per outer turn; line 188 moves at most once per call, and the normal carrier branch calls movement at most once per turn. Carrier action cooldown is 8 and empty movement cooldown is 5 (RobotType.CARRIER and specs line 106), so legal second actions/moves sometimes exist. HQ already loops its low-cooldown actions, demonstrating this is an omitted carrier optimization rather than an engine restriction.
4. **Navigation and combat are intentionally shallow.** Lines 174–188 implement greedy neighboring movement with a short taboo penalty, no stay-put option, no terrain/current planning, and no completeness guarantee. Carriers choose wells by home-distance rather than route accessibility and do not revalidate well resource type after a transformation (49, 101–108). Launchers prioritize enemy launchers and avoid sensed HQ radius but walk toward opponents without range management, concentration, retreat, island control, or health awareness (145–183). These are quality ceilings, not proven crashes. Successful easy-opponent matches do not discharge them.
5. **Fixed and opportunistic anchor policy.** Lines 71–77 reserve one stockpiled anchor after round 120; lines 98–99 send any eligible empty adjacent miner to deliver it. There is no outstanding-delivery accounting, carrier role allocation, or neutral-island demand check. Repeated pickups can divert the entire finite workforce; carriers holding anchors continue exploring if no neutral island exists. This is a plausible adverse state supported by code, not yet a demonstrated replay failure.

## Style and decomposition
Positive: descriptive role-sized methods, only the required API and plain Java, modest state, bounded loops, no external dependency clutter, no obvious copied lecture structure, clear catch diagnostics and guaranteed yield. At 191 lines a single class is reasonable. Negative: compressed one-line control flow and magic numbers (40, 4096, 8192, 20, 55, 120, 160) obscure invariants; `attackTarget` has a misleading query-like name although it fires and its return is unused. Communication encoding, movement scoring, and economic policy would benefit from named constants and short invariant comments. The generic exception catch preserves liveness but does not ensure useful behavior or exception-free turns.

## Rule understanding
The initial plan correctly identifies indestructible HQs, isolated stockpiles, carrying and delivery, anchor weight/cost, permission-limited shared writes, action/movement independence, and tiebreak sequence. Its shorthand “island majority wins” is imprecise: the actual instant threshold is 75%, not a simple majority (specs lines 66, 160). The implemented strategy does not itself hardcode the wrong threshold, so this wording is not an observed gameplay bug.

## Opponent caveats
The official lecture opponent is active but visibly flawed, limiting any broad conclusions from wins. Communication.updateIslandInfo passes an array index (`id + STARTING_ISLAND_IDX`) into bitPackIslandInfo as if it were an island ID. readTeamHoldingIsland computes `% 0b1`, always zero. CarrierStrategy iterates already-offset island indices and passes them to a reader which adds the offset again. Carrier island destinations are not invalidated after capture; this can make anchor carriers head toward an already-owned island indefinitely. Launcher targeting does not exclude indestructible HQs, and the HQ waits for 300 mana before a launcher burst. These source issues should not be silently repaired for this diagnostic, nor should their precise measured effect be assumed without replay evidence.

## Focused modification and experiment review
The full 208-line standoff variant was reviewed by comparing the immutable build snapshots. Its exact diff changes only baseline line 152 into in-range launcher-specific dispatch and adds the 14-line `standoff` helper (variant lines 167–180). All remaining source is identical. This is a suitably narrow intervention, though bypassing moveToward also omits its recent-position history update/penalty for those turns; that is part of the policy replacement.

**Variant-specific correctness concern:** the stay-put score at line 171 is only distance error, whereas candidate move scores at lines 174–176 include HQ danger. This asymmetry can retain a launcher inside HQ range even when a legal safer escape exists. For example, already being at desired distance13 gives stay score0, and no move can beat it even if the current square receives HQ damage. The code's claim of a strong HQ penalty therefore does not apply to staying. No illegal API use results; this is a tactical scoring bug and has not been isolated in replay.

**Additional shared carrier bug verified against engine implementation:** initial lines 91 and 106–108 require weight40 before returning from a distant well and call collectResource(well,-1). In pinned official RobotControllerImpl.java lines 934–940, -1 is replaced by the *well's rate*, then rejected if the inventory cannot fit that rate. At an upgraded rate3 well a carrier can reach weight39, fail every further max-rate collection, and neither move nor return while adjacent to the well. The packaged API description “max possible” alone obscures this edge case. Specs describe rate3 upgraded wells; engine Well.getRate confirms it. The candidate and educational opponent do not themselves upgrade wells, so this is an adversarial/generalization defect rather than an established failure of the eight diagnostic games.

The script's computation matches these checks. The engine records every surviving robot's end-round position (GameWorld.java710–713), including stationary robots; the metric is consequently a survivor-position exposure measure, not a movement-event count. The preregistration and script correctly use position geometry and disclaim visibility/current/within-round causal interpretations. Survivor-conditioned exposure can change with deaths and encounters, so the denominator is not an independent sample and the metric cannot prove the mechanism by itself. The encounter floor mitigates but does not eliminate selection effects.

Minor analysis robustness issues: `max(1, denominator)` displays no-exposure as0.0 rather than undefined; the preregistration correctly excludes baseline DefaultB, but a future condition with zero variant exposure could count as a reduction unless handled explicitly. The script groups by round rather than (match,round), relying on this tool's single-map/single-match outputs; it also assumes team IDs1/2 rather than deriving them from metadata. These assumptions hold in every inspected artifact. Reusing this script on a multi-match replay would require fixes. It prints source hashes but does not itself assert equality across the three slices; my independent verification did so.

## Overall assessment
**Strong bounded experimental reasoning and honest evidence; competent compact baseline code with significant unresolved strategic/state edge cases.** The entrant found an actionable local behavior, implemented one controlled change, used properly scoped replay geometry, defined meaningful floors and rejection conditions, retained counterevidence, and selected consistently. The strongest positive signal is that chain of observable engineering work, not eight wins against the teaching bot. Code remains intentionally simple, with no workforce survival accounting, persistent discovery delivery, complete navigation, or multi-enemy combat planning. The upgraded-well deadlock and asymmetric stay-hazard score are concrete correctness concerns beyond ordinary strategic incompleteness. This is a credible diagnostic success, not a demonstration of tournament-ready strength. No review finding or suggested fix was sent to the competitor, and no competitor-area file was changed by this reviewer.
