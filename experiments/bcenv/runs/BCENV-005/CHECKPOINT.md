# BCENV-005 completed practical prototype checkpoint

## Established

The existing VM successfully hosted a bounded development service for a fresh autonomous competitor. It produced eight legal versions and 64 real development games, selected an immutable version itself, and stopped before the independent new evaluation panel was acquired. No observer edited or coached its strategy.

The blind review was fixed before evaluation scores: source-bound prospective predictions, rejected experiments, 53 authenticated replay-feedback receipts covering 14 complete intervals, and independently rerun actual-candidate Java tests support a credible observable learning loop. Real replay actions demonstrate economic and anchor-delivery progress, not just winning labels. Correctness remains partial; the review records concrete stale-state, resource-typing and current-safety risks without attributing them to losses.

The fixed independent evaluation completed **24/24 valid games, 9 wins and 15 losses**, no draws, retries, timeouts or infrastructure failures. Every game ended by island capture and both teams showed active spawning, movement and actions. Source and binary inventories remained unchanged. The aggregate file and blind review accompany this checkpoint.

This is a credible working competitive-environment prototype with a nontrivial autonomous bot, not proof of general competitive reliability or tournament strength. No win-rate acceptance threshold was preregistered and none is invented after results. Different panels/maps and multiple environment changes prevent interpreting 9/24 versus the prior 4/24 as a controlled improvement.

## Limits and remaining decisions

- Native same-user tools are procedurally restricted, not technically confined. Full native access traces are unavailable; absence of unauthorized reads is not proven. Future evaluation sources were materialized only after stop. This does not meet a hostile-tenant or hard six-tool confinement claim.
- Public historical source provenance is pinned, but exact final-server-upload equivalence and unknown model pretraining exposure remain limitations.
- One successful notes/test-profile run does not establish fresh-agent repeatability. Its now-observed evaluation panel must be labeled regression data if reused.
- Actual-code mocks are useful but do not reproduce full engine cooldown/current/bytecode/concurrency semantics.
- Rejected request attempts are incompletely logged; successful read counts are lower bounds. Runtime process bounds are not aggregate cgroup quotas.

All candidate/service/evaluator work is stopped. No additional trial is queued and no PR is merged. Two proportionate next scopes are **fresh-agent replication** under a separately committed untouched panel, to test repeatability; or **engineering/release hardening** of audit/restart documentation and explicitly scoped access enforcement, without additional strategy experiments. These are future scope choices, not current setup blockers.
